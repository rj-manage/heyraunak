“A jewel is a promise written in light — and signed, twice, by the hands that made it.— A. Éclat, founder”

Maison Éclat began in a single room above a Parisian watchmaker’s, where Auguste Éclat set his first commissioned piece — a sapphire hairpin for a dancer who could not pay in full, and instead paid in stories. He kept the stories. The maison still does.

Every Éclat piece carries a bench mark no client ever sees: the initials of the artisan who set the final stone. It is our way of saying that a jewel, like a letter, should always be signed.

Maison notes

Pages from the ledger

Leaf through three entries from the maison’s private journal. Tap a year to open it.

Auguste Éclat opened his bench with a single purchase: six grams of scrap gold and a loan of tools from the watchmaker upstairs. His first commission, the sapphire hairpin, is still in the family — returned to the maison in 1989 by the dancer’s granddaughter, who asked only that it be cleaned and told again.

A collector from Bombay ordered a parure of forty-one stones and forbade the maison from rushing. Eleven months later the atelier delivered a necklace that folded into a box the size of a matchbook. The collector’s only recorded remark: “It will do. It will do very well indeed.”

When smoke filled the Paris workshop, the artisans carried out every piece before a single stone was saved — records and casts were recovered last. The fire took the roof. It did not take a single setting. We rebuilt the bench exactly as it stood, and we have not moved it since.
